      How well it does on each digit.

      Precision: when the model says 3, how often it really is a 3.

      Recall: of all the real 3s, how many the model calls 3.

      F1: one number that balances the two.

      From your pen to a guess

      1. You draw on a white square. When you lift your pen, your browser finds the edges of your drawing, centres it in a square and shrinks it to 28 × 28 grey pixels, the same format as the training images.
      2. Only that small image is sent to the server. Your full drawing never leaves your browser.
      3. The server runs a convolutional neural network. Two convolution layers pick out strokes, corners and curves. Two dense layers turn those features into a score for each digit.
      4. The scores become ten probabilities that add up to 100%. The highest one is the guess, and the bars show the rest.
      5. The image is discarded after the guess. Nothing is saved or used for training.

      The network learned from MNIST, a public set of 70,000 handwritten digits written by American high school students and census staff. It has never seen your handwriting, so unusual styles can fool it. Try a 7 with a crossbar, or a 1 with a long base.
